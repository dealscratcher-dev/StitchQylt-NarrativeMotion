# Stories That Move
### An immersive reader that adds *pace*—without turning text into video.

Most reading on the internet is flat.

Not because writing is bad—  
but because the medium doesn’t help.

A long page has no pacing. No guidance. No “breathing room.”  
You either skim, you drown, or you forget it five minutes later.

**Stories That Move** is a prototype that explores a quieter idea:

> **Motion should support comprehension—  
> like atmosphere for meaning—  
> not replace the text with images.**

---

## What this is (in one line)

A reader that watches what’s on-screen as you scroll and adds subtle motion layers—  
so the page feels like it has *beats*.

---

## What you’ll notice

As you read, the system tries to add a few quiet layers:

- **Beats** → content is segmented into meaningful sections (not random chopping)
- **Tone + intensity** → mood and strength shift the “scene”
- **Motion overlays** → visuals respond to attention, but don’t hijack it
- **Scroll sync** → the scene evolves as you move through the page

Think of it like **subtitles for the internet**, except instead of subtitles for audio—  
it’s **atmosphere for meaning**.

---

## Try it

1. Paste any article URL (blog / news / essay)
2. Click **Load**
3. Click **Read**
4. Scroll—and watch the motion adapt

**Tip:** start with a clean article (minimal popups / heavy paywalls).

---

## What counts as a win

This is early, so success criteria are simple:

- It loads reliably (no broken pages)
- Motion feels reactive, **not distracting**
- Segmentation feels like real “beats”
- It works across different writing styles and layouts
